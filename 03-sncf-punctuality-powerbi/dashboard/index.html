TGV Punctuality Dashboard

SNCF Voyageurs open data: monthly TGV regularity by route, January 2018 to September 2026

How punctual is the TGV, and where does it break down?

3.4 million train runs on 146 routes over 105 months. Use the filters above to slice the whole dashboard by service, axis, journey length and year. Click a bar in the Overview to filter by axis.

Punctuality is getting worse after a post-2021 lowLate arrivals peaked at 17.8% of trains in 2018, fell to 11.3% in 2021 (a year of reduced traffic), then rose again. For January to September, 2026 sits at 18.7% against 14.3% in 2025.
When a train is late, it is later than beforeThe average delay of a late train grew from 30.7 minutes (2018) to 40.4 (2026, January to September). Trains over 60 minutes late went from 1.5% to 3.1% of runs between 2021 and 2026.
Summer is the weak seasonJuly has the highest late rate of the year (18.5%) and the most severe delays, against 12.2% in March.
Cross-country routes are the least reliableRoutes that do not touch Paris are late 19.5% of the time, against 14.0% for routes with a Paris terminal.
Cancellations spike with disruptionApril 2020 shows 53.6% of planned trains cancelled, April 2021 37.5% and April 2018 32.7%, in line with the lockdowns and the spring 2018 rail strikes.
No single cause dominatesExternal causes (22%), traffic management (21%), infrastructure (21%) and rolling stock (19%) each explain about a fifth of late trains.

Method and limits

Source: SNCF Voyageurs, Régularité mensuelle TGV par liaisons, Open Database Licence (ODbL). Cleaning rules and row counts are in docs/DATA_QUALITY_LOG.md.

Definitions: late arrival = the source's count of trains late at arrival. SNCF counts a train as on time within 5 min (journeys under 1h30), 10 min (1h30 to 3h) or 15 min (over 3h), and I read the column that way, but the dataset does not document it precisely. Rates are ratios of sums: late arrivals divided by trains run, cancellations divided by trains planned.

Important: a TGV can serve several routes, so it is counted once on each. The totals here are route-level movements, not the official SNCF network or axis regularity, which SNCF publishes separately. Cause shares are an estimate (each route-month's cause percentages applied to its late trains). The axis is derived from the Paris terminal of each route, not a field in the source.
